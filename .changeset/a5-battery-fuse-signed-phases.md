The home battery now protects each fuse phase in the direction its current flows. It no longer discharges into a phase that already exports past the fuse, and no longer cuts a discharge that keeps an importing phase under it. It counts its own share of each phase, so it holds a steady relief instead of switching between full relief and none on every dispatch, also when its own power reading lags the meter. When one phase imports past the line and another exports past it, it takes the move that leaves the worse phase least over instead of holding its last command. When the meter does not show its moves, because the meter repeats one reading or the battery sits on a phase other than the one over the line, it stops adding relief once it has moved one ampere per phase, instead of running out to its discharge limit. A battery on another phase then goes back to its power from before that relief, so it does not hold its own phase past the line. A load that cancels its move on a live meter does not stop it, nor do phase currents that a whole-ampere meter rounds to the same value while its total moves. It relieves to the fuse less `safety_margin_a`, the same line the car uses.
