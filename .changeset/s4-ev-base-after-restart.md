After a restart, the home battery no longer stays in discharge for a car the meter already shows charging. Until the charger's first order of the new run can have landed, the battery counts the car's measured draw as part of the meter reading, never above FTW's latest order to the car and never above the consumption the site meter shows. FTW does not know the charger's offer in force then: one from before the restart, under an older max_charge_w, or the charger's own default. So the battery still keeps room for the car to rise from its draw to the larger of the charger's max_charge_w and the site fuse's rating. On a site whose fuse allows more than the car's draw plus FTW's latest order, that leaves no credit; a charger with no max_charge_w gets none either. The draw counts only when the charger stamped a sample at least 5 s, and at most one control interval more, before the site reading arrived, and its last poll succeeded. The 5 s cover a charger clock running behind FTW's, as OCPP chargers stamp their own samples. The lowest sample since then counts, so a rise the meter does not show yet never does. A charger that samples every 30 s or more rarely gets the credit. Before, the 0 A orders FTW sent while the meter was still starting counted as the car's draw, so a lower order looked like a rise and the battery kept discharging toward its floor.
